Interview Angle
"Walk me through B2B vs B2C" — lead with sales cycle length, who the buyer is vs. who the user is, and how pricing + retention differ. Give a real example of each (Salesforce vs. Netflix).
Founder Angle
Pick the model that matches your distribution — if you can reach buyers directly and cheaply, D2C/PLG wins; if the sale needs trust and customization, B2B sales-led wins even if it's slower.

Core Business Models (Who You Sell To)

Market Structure
Primary Types
B2BBusiness sells to businesses — Salesforce, AWS, SAP
B2CBusiness sells to individual consumers — Netflix, Amazon retail
B2B2CBusiness sells via another business to reach end consumers — Shopify (powers merchants' stores), Stripe
D2CBrand sells directly to consumers, skipping retailers — Warby Parker, Dollar Shave Club
C2CPlatform connects consumers to consumers — eBay, OLX, Craigslist
B2GBusiness sells to government agencies — Palantir, defense/civic tech
C2BConsumers offer value businesses buy — freelance marketplaces, UGC platforms
P2PPlatform enables peer-to-peer exchange, takes a cut — Airbnb, Uber
Most real companies are hybrids: Uber is C2C/P2P for rides but B2B for Uber for Business accounts.

B2B vs B2C at a Glance

Head-to-Head Comparison
DimensionB2BB2C
BuyerCommittee — user ≠ buyer ≠ budget holderIndividual — user = buyer, usually
Sales cycleWeeks to 18+ monthsSeconds to days
Deal sizeFew to many $100Ks/yearLow, high volume
Decision driverROI, risk reduction, complianceEmotion, convenience, price, trend
Marketing channelSales reps, events, LinkedIn, ABMSocial ads, SEO, influencers, app stores
Retention leverContracts, switching cost, supportHabit, brand loyalty, network effects
Pricing modelNegotiated, tiered/seat-basedFixed, published, take-it-or-leave-it
B2B optimizes for fewer, bigger, stickier deals; B2C optimizes for volume and velocity.

Revenue Models (How You Charge)

Monetization
Subscription / SaaSRecurring fee for ongoing access — predictable MRR
FreemiumFree core tier, paid upgrade for power features/limits
Marketplace commission% cut of every transaction between two sides
Usage-basedPay per API call / GB / seat-hour — scales with customer value
LicensingOne-time or per-seat fee for IP/software rights
AdvertisingFree product, revenue from attention/data sold to advertisers
Affiliate / referralCommission for driving a sale elsewhere
Hybrid stacking is common: Amazon = D2C retail + marketplace commission + AWS SaaS/usage-based + Prime subscription.

Platform & Network Effects

Why Some Businesses Compound
Platform Types
Two-sided marketplaceConnects distinct supply + demand sides — Airbnb, Upwork
AggregatorOwns the customer relationship, commoditizes suppliers — Google, Uber
Platform (vs. product)Lets 3rd parties build on top — App Store, Shopify apps
Network Effect Types
DirectMore users → more value for same-side users (WhatsApp)
Indirect / cross-sideMore of one side → more value for the other side (riders/drivers)
Data network effectMore usage → better algorithm → better product (recommendation engines)
Chicken-and-egg problem: marketplaces must bootstrap one side first (often subsidized) before network effects kick in.

Unit Economics & Key Metrics

The Numbers That Matter
CACCustomer Acquisition Cost — total sales+marketing spend ÷ new customers
LTVLifetime Value — total gross margin a customer generates over their life
LTV:CACTarget ≥ 3:1 — below that, growth is unprofitable
Payback periodMonths to recover CAC from gross margin — under 12 is healthy for SaaS
Churn rate% of customers/revenue lost per period — logo churn vs. revenue churn
NRRNet Revenue Retention — expansion minus churn from existing customers; >100% = grows without new sales
MRR / ARRMonthly / Annual Recurring Revenue — the SaaS heartbeat metric
Gross marginRevenue minus cost to serve — software ~80%+, marketplaces ~20-40%
Burn multipleNet burn ÷ net new ARR — lower is more capital-efficient
Conversion rateFree→paid or visitor→customer % — the funnel's core lever

Go-To-Market (GTM) Strategies

How You Acquire Customers
Product-led (PLG)Free trial/freemium sells itself — Slack, Figma, Notion
Sales-ledReps drive the deal — high-touch, works for big B2B ACV
Marketing-ledContent/SEO/ads generate inbound demand before sales engages
Channel / partner-ledResellers or integrations distribute the product for you
Community-ledOrganic advocacy & word-of-mouth drive adoption — open source, dev tools
Low price + low complexity → PLG. High price + high complexity/risk → sales-led. Most scaled companies blend motions over time.
